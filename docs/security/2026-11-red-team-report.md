## Summary

**Verdict: GO, conditional.** The review found no Critical, High or Medium finding. Eight Low findings remain — one, **F-C3-4a**, is analytical and unconfirmed and carries a production-gating caveat (its Low severity rests on an unconfirmed assumption about the live Zama ACL; if the real ACL is looser than the mock it escalates to Critical) — and several privacy wordings in the README and the app overstate how secret things are. Nothing found blocks a Sepolia testnet deploy. Conditions apply: run the Sepolia-only checks after a testnet deploy; **before any production/mainnet reliance**, run the production-gating D-C3-4 / F-C3-4a ACL confirmation and upgrade `@openzeppelin/confidential-contracts` to 0.4.2 (the GHSA-29h2-jr22-frmh fix; `package.json` was frozen so the red team could not apply it in-branch); and decide on the cheap fixes listed below, because the contracts cannot be changed after deployment.

### What was tested and how

- Ten areas: deposits and theft (C2), confidentiality and inference (C3), griefing, gas and schedule maths (C4), and deployment, configuration and the keeper script (C5).
- 11 test files under `test/redteam/` plus one shared base (Foundry and Hardhat). The final run, at commit `71b53f5`: `forge test` 221 passed, 0 failed; `npx hardhat test` 101 passing, 0 failing. Every attack was really attempted; a "Held" result needs a test that would have failed if the attack had worked.
- Solvency invariant: random sequences of mint, top-up, accrue, claim, transfer, payout request and finalise across both NFT contracts and all three tokens (7,680 calls per run, zero reverts). After every call the decrypted books balance: the contract always holds at least what it owes, and nobody is paid more than was deposited.
- Confidentiality tests: for each role (creator, donor, owner, previous owner, operator, requester, outsider) which encrypted values it can read, checked with the real decryption plugin; a search of every log, return value and calldata for the secret amounts (none on the wire); and a predictor that tries to work out hidden amounts from public data only.

### By-design behaviours users must be warned about

These are accepted by the specs, but the current wording and app screens do not make them clear.

- **What automatic payouts reveal over time.** Each payout amount is public. A short payout shows the endowment ran out, and the sum of all payouts is then exactly the secret original endowment. A donation made after the token runs dry, and any leftover when a fixed schedule ends, become public with the next payout. Even without automatic payouts, a claim amount can usually be worked out from the public schedule; what stays hidden is the remaining endowment and whether a claim was capped (D-C3-3).
- **An under-funded ENFT looks exactly like a funded one (D-C2-2, D-C4-7a).** A creator can mint an ENFT with nothing in it. Nobody, including the buyer, can check. Only the creator's word vouches for it.
- **Creators can prove, not only claim, what they funded (D-C3-4).** This reasoning is analytical and needs confirmation by a Zama or FHEVM expert. One dimension of it — an immutable-mode creator forcing a transferred-away token's remaining endowment public — is now carried as the named Low finding **F-C3-4a**, with a production-gating ACL confirmation (it escalates to Critical if the real ACL is looser than the mock).
- **No admin, no pause, no upgrade (D-C5-6).** A fix after deployment means new contracts and no migration of existing ENFTs.
- **Donation order (D-C4-8):** after a token runs dry, a donation may be claimable at once or stream forward, depending on whether someone accrues first.
- **Keepers (D-C4-2, D-C4-2b):** an unfunded token costs a keeper two transactions once; do not set `KEEPER_INCLUDE_DRY` unattended.

### Checks that can only be done on Sepolia

Several results rest on the local mock and **must be confirmed on Sepolia after deploying**, using small amounts. The "Sepolia-only checks" section says how, with a pass condition for each:

- HCU (computation) limits on the heavy paths: `mint`, `topUp`, `claim`, `requestPayout`, and a transfer of a long-scheduled token. Local tests lifted some caps.
- KMS decryption timing and real proof formats for `finalizePayout`.
- The host-contract `getVersion()` match with `@fhevm/solidity` 0.11.1 (not confirmed).
- The real-ACL confirmation of D-C3-4 / F-C3-4a (transient access by recomputation, and the immutable-mode public-disclosure dimension). F-C2-4a was listed here at review; since its fix the contract refuses an empty proof itself, whatever the real verifier does. **This D-C3-4 / F-C3-4a check is gating for production, not the owner's call:** it must be run on the real network before any production/mainnet reliance. If the real ACL is looser than the mock — it grants the recomputed handle more freely than assumed, or lets a non-creator reconstruct a value it does not already know — treat F-C3-4a as Critical. It stays non-blocking for a Sepolia testnet deploy (nothing of value is at risk there).
- The canonical-WETH 2300-gas forward (no fork was possible). The `receive()` needs about 95 gas, so a wide margin is expected.
- Also, as a **pre-production condition** (not merely a Sepolia line item): upgrade `@openzeppelin/confidential-contracts` from 0.4.1 to 0.4.2, the GHSA-29h2-jr22-frmh fix (`package.json` was frozen so the red team could not apply it in-branch). ### Go / no-go

The review found no Critical, High or Medium finding. The solvency invariant and the endowment-confidentiality property both hold. The contracts have no admin role and no upgrade path, so this is the one chance to fix anything before deploy.

**Recommendation: GO, conditional on** (a) the owner running the Sepolia-only checks after deploying to a testnet and before relying on it, (b) deciding on each Low finding's cheap fix and the privacy-wording fixes, and (c) **two pre-production conditions before any production/mainnet reliance: run the production-gating D-C3-4 / F-C3-4a ACL confirmation (F-C3-4a escalates to Critical if the real ACL is looser than the mock), and upgrade `@openzeppelin/confidential-contracts` to 0.4.2 (the GHSA-29h2-jr22-frmh fix; the frozen `package.json` blocked applying it in-branch; its exposure is bounded and non-blocking for a testnet deploy).** The Low findings and the wording are the owner's call, not blockers for a Sepolia testnet deploy. When the review was delivered no fix had been applied and all were proposals for the owner to approve. F-C2-4a and F-C4-1 have since been fixed (see "Fixes applied after the review").

## Finding details

### F-C2-4a: Deposits accept an existing handle with an empty input proof

- **Severity:** Low (surprising but harmless: only the caller's own funds move, and the caller learns nothing it could not already learn).
- **Affected:** `EndowmentNFT.mint` and `EndowmentNFT.topUp`, and `AutoPayoutEndowmentNFT.mintAuto` through `_mintEndowment` (ET:82, ET:219).
- **Status: fixed after the review.** `mint`, `mintAuto` and `topUp` now revert `EmptyInputProof` when the proof is empty. The rest of this entry describes the contracts as reviewed.
- **PoC tests:** `test_C2_4a_ownDistributionAsInput_emptyProof_reverts`, `test_C2_4a_previousOwnerOldDistributionAsInput_emptyProof_reverts`, `test_C2_4a_operatorTransientHandleAsInput_emptyProof_reverts` in `test/redteam/Theft.t.sol`. At review these were named `test_FINDING_C2_4a_*` and showed the deposit accepted; since the fix they assert the revert. `test_C2_4_existingHandleAsInput_reverts`, cited below, now sees `EmptyInputProof` too.
- **What happens:** `FHE.fromExternal(h, proof)` with `proof.length == 0` does not verify an input. It treats `h` as an existing handle and checks only that `msg.sender`, here the NFT contract's caller, is allowed on it (`@fhevm/solidity` FHE.sol:8598-8609; a zero `h` becomes a trivial 0). The NFT contract then calls `FHE.allowTransient(amount, token)` (ET:83, ET:220), which the host ACL allows only if the NFT contract itself holds access (`test_C2_4_existingHandleAsInput_reverts` sees `ACL.SenderNotAllowed(enft)` when it does not). The amount then goes into `confidentialTransferFrom(msg.sender, this, amount)`, whose `T:147` check the NFT contract passes for the same reason. So the usable handles are exactly those on which both the caller and the NFT contract hold access, persistent or transient in the same transaction. Funds always come from the caller (`from` is `msg.sender`, ET:84, ET:221); the handle only sets the amount.
- **The usable set, from the Access table and the tests, and whether the caller already knows the value** (a lower bound — see the caveat below and C3-4 in Task 5)**:**
  - The caller's own tokens' current `distribution` (ET:98-99, :238, :278, :291, :257): known, since the caller is the owner and can user-decrypt it. Tested (`..._ownDistributionAsInput_...`).
  - `distribution` handles from tokens the caller owned before (never revoked): known, because the caller held them while owner. Tested (`..._previousOwnerOldDistributionAsInput_...`): the old handle still carries `persistAllowed` for the previous owner, it is accepted, and the new owner's distribution is untouched.
  - The shared trivial-zero handle (`asEuint64(0)`, ET:90, :236): known (0).
  - `transferred`/`received` from the caller's own deposits (to the NFT contract: T:305-307; the caller is `from`): known. The caller can decrypt it, and as creator it chose the amount.
  - `transferred` from the caller's own claims (from the NFT contract to the owner: T:305-306): known, for the same reason.
  - The payout `unwrapAmount_` (W:218): public, but the caller holds no ACL on it (public decryption is not `isAllowed`), so it is not usable.
  - `endowmentRemaining`, `actual`, and the NFT contract's own balance: not reachable by passing the **stored** handle directly — the caller holds no ACL on it (`SenderNotAllowedToUseHandle`, tested).
  - **Caveat (lower bound, not the whole set):** the list above counts only handles the caller already holds ACL on. The set of handles a caller can *use at call time* is larger, because a handle derived purely from inputs a party itself holds can be reached by other means available to that party; whether and how that extends to `endowmentRemaining`/`actual` (for example for a creator, from data it already has) is a confidentiality question assessed under C3-4 in Task 5, not on the deposit path here. It does not change this finding's Low severity: on the deposit path the funds still come only from the caller (`from` is `msg.sender`), and the deeper question is whether such access could be used to disclose a value to others without the owner opting in, which Task 5 (C3-4, C3-5) owns.
  - **Transient grants in the same transaction:** the token grants the caller transient access to `transferred` in `confidentialTransferFrom` (T:138, T:150) and to `wrappedAmountSent` in `wrap` (W:88). The NFT contract also holds `transferred` only when it is `from` or `to`. It is `from` only in its own calls, where the caller of the token is the NFT contract. So the case that remains is a caller acting as operator of a third party X, moving X's tokens to the NFT contract. Tested (`..._operatorTransientHandleAsInput_...`). The caller holds only a transient grant on that `transferred` (no `persistAllowed`), so it cannot user-decrypt it. Its value is the amount the caller chose, or 0 if X was short, and that value now shows up in the caller's own balance. **The caller did not know this value beforehand.** It learns one bit about X's balance (at least the amount, or not), and X's tokens sit in the NFT contract's pool, credited to no token. The operator right X granted already gives the caller the same bit and the same power over X's funds: it could transfer from X to itself and decrypt its own balance. So the deposit path adds nothing beyond that right, and the severity is left at Low. `wrap` to the NFT contract gives a plaintext-derived amount the caller chose, which is known.
- **Impact in plain words:** anyone may size a deposit by a handle they can already use, instead of a fresh encrypted input. They pay it themselves. Nobody else's funds move through the NFT contract, and no value is revealed that the caller could not already obtain.
- **Suggested fix:** in `_mintEndowment` and `topUp`, `if (inputProof.length == 0) revert EmptyInputProof();` before `FHE.fromExternal`. Alternatively keep the behaviour and document it. It is documented upstream behaviour of `FHE.fromExternal`, meant for composability, since a contract caller can then pass a handle it computed. The owner can weigh rejecting empty proofs (simpler, closer to the spec, which mentions only `fromExternal(enc, proof)` at spec lines 105 and 128) against keeping it for contract callers that deposit computed amounts.

### F-C3-4a: Immutable-mode creator can force the current owner's remaining endowment public

- **Severity:** Low. The only party able to do this is the creator, and only in immutable mode, only with no third-party top-ups, and only after the creator has transferred the token away (creator ≠ current owner). What is disclosed is the value of the creator's own original immutable-mode contribution — equivalently the current remaining endowment, which in immutable mode is that contribution minus the public, already-derivable accruals — a value the creator always knew and could already compute and attest, and which D-C2-2 already records as vouched for by the creator's word alone. It is **not** an arbitrary third party forcing an unknown amount public (which would be the High band, "a hidden amount forced public without the owner opting in"). See the Critical-escalation caveat under the severity rationale: this Low rests on an unconfirmed assumption about the live Zama ACL.
- **Affected:** `EndowmentNFT`, the `endowmentRemaining` handle, reached via FHEVM handle recomputation (a party holding ACL on every operand can re-derive the content-addressed result handle — the general mechanism is D-C3-4) followed by `FHE.makePubliclyDecryptable` / `ACL.allowForDecryption`. Immutable mode only; creator ≠ current owner.
- **PoC test:** None. Analytical; no runnable PoC — the exploit construction was stopped by a safety classifier; it was not reworded or retried. Needs manual expert confirmation. (Consistent with C3-4 / D-C3-4, which were deliberately documented analytically rather than as a runnable exploit.)
- **Scope (precise):**
  - **Immutable mode only.** With no third-party top-ups, the operand chain from the creator's own `received` through the public accrual chain reconstructs the current `endowmentRemaining`. Once a third party tops up, the chain breaks — the creator lacks ACL on the donor's `received` operand (ET:223) — and the current remaining is no longer reconstructable. D-C3-4 and C3-2, and the Confidentiality / Task-5 evidence, already establish this.
  - **Only when creator ≠ current owner** (the creator has transferred the token to someone else). The disclosure then falls on the current owner (the transferee), not on the creator itself.
  - The disclosed value is the creator's own original immutable-mode contribution, which the creator always knew and which D-C2-2 already says only the creator's word vouches for — so **no value is revealed that the creator could not already compute and attest.**
- **The tension (stated plainly; neither clause is privileged).** Spec §3 gives two co-equal clauses that pull in opposite directions here:
  - "Always hidden from: the public, and any owner who received the NFT by transfer."
  - "In Immutable mode: the original creator knows the initial endowment (they encrypted it) and can compute remaining from the public schedule + elapsed time until exhaustion."
  The first implies the current owner (a transferee) should keep the ability to hold the remaining endowment private from the public. The second grants the immutable-mode creator knowledge of that same remaining. The behaviour here lets the creator act on the second clause to **remove** the ability the first clause implies for the current owner: by recomputing `endowmentRemaining` and calling `makePubliclyDecryptable`, the creator can force the current owner's remaining endowment public. The two §3 clauses are therefore in tension; this report records both and does not silently privilege one over the other.
- **Severity rationale (Low):** the only party who can do this is the creator, disclosing only its own immutable-mode contribution (a value it already knew); it is not an arbitrary third party forcing an unknown amount public, which is why it sits at Low rather than in the High band. **But this rests on an unconfirmed assumption that the live Zama ACL/coprocessor gates transient-ACL-by-recomputation as the local mock does. If the real ACL grants the recomputed handle more freely than assumed — or lets a non-creator reconstruct a value they do not already know — this escalates to Critical. The owner (as spec owner) must either bless "in immutable mode the creator may publicly disclose the endowment they funded" so the spec and behaviour agree, or treat this as a higher finding.**
- **Impact in plain words:** in immutable mode, a creator who has given a token away can make that token's remaining endowment public, removing the current owner's ability to keep it private — but only reveals the amount the creator itself funded and already knew.
- **Suggested fix / disposition:** this is analytical and unconfirmed. Confirm the ACL semantics on the real network before any production reliance (see the Sepolia-only checks, where this D-C3-4 / F-C3-4a check is gating for production). No runnable exploit or step-by-step reconstruction recipe is given, deliberately. If the owner blesses immutable-mode creator disclosure, amend spec §3 so its "always hidden from: the public" wording and the behaviour agree; otherwise treat as a higher finding.
- **Owner's decision (2026-11-15, after the review):** blessed. Spec §3 was amended: the contract never discloses the remaining balance, and a creator may disclose the endowment they funded. The Sepolia ACL check is unchanged and still gates production.

### F-C4-1: Unbounded schedule length

- **Severity:** Low. No on-chain lock is possible; the extra gas is self-inflicted and bounded, and the main residual is an off-chain indexer surface.
- **Affected:** `EndowmentNFT._storeSchedule` (ET:107-122) bounds segment durations and total end, but **not the number of segments**. Every `accrue`/`claim`/`transferFrom` copies the whole schedule from storage into memory and loops it (`Schedule.integrate` + `Schedule.scheduleEnd`, ET:261-294, :241-259), as does `AutoPayoutEndowmentNFT.requestPayout` (AP:151-177).
- **Status: fixed after the review.** A schedule is capped at 256 segments (`MAX_SEGMENTS`); `mint` and `mintAuto` revert `TooManySegments` above that. The rest of this entry, including the gas table up to 1000 segments, describes the contracts as reviewed; those sizes can no longer be minted.
- **PoC tests:** `test_C4_1_gasScaling_mintIsTheCeiling`, `test_C4_1_largestMintableToken_staysCheapToUse` (`test/redteam/Griefing.t.sol`). At review the first measured up to 1000 segments and the second (then `…_staysClaimableAndTransferable`) minted 1211; since the fix both stop at 256, and `test_C4_1_scheduleAboveSegmentCap_reverts` checks the cap.
- **The headline DoS does NOT occur.** Measured gas on the forge-fhevm mock (each segment is 1e6 s at 1 unit/s; accrual 10 s into the schedule):

  | segments | mint | accrue | claim | transferFrom | mintAuto | requestPayout |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 723,873 | 140,829 | 354,646 | 112,511 | 710,663 | 565,177 |
  | 10 | 833,490 | 152,368 | 362,773 | 96,738 | 839,388 | 576,716 |
  | 100 | 2,984,043 | 268,381 | 444,670 | 178,638 | 2,991,522 | 692,740 |
  | 1000 | 24,538,941 | 1,491,165 | 1,326,291 | 1,060,279 | 24,596,722 | 1,915,630 |

  `mint` SSTOREs every segment (~23,776 gas per added segment), roughly 15-20x the SLOAD-bound per-segment cost of a read operation. So **mint is the binding constraint**: the largest schedule a single `mint` fits in one block is ~**1231 segments at a 30,000,000 limit** and ~**2493 at 60,000,000**. Minting at the 30M ceiling (1211 segments, mint 29,648,289 gas) still leaves the token fully operable inside the SAME 30M block: `accrue` 1,794,865, `claim` 1,582,860, `transferFrom` 1,307,540 — about 5% of the block each. A creator CAN mint such a token straight to a victim (`to`); the victim claims it and passes it on in the test. It is **never un-claimable or un-transferable**, so the C4-1 brick criterion is not met: the denial-of-service is **Held**. HCU is independent of length (the schedule maths is plaintext; each real call does a constant handful of FHE operations), so there is no HCU brick either.
- **The Low residual (why this is still recorded).** Two real, bounded effects remain: (1) a creator can push an arbitrarily large schedule onto a victim, who then pays amplified gas (~1.3M vs ~113k for a one-segment token, ~12x) merely to transfer it away — self-inflicted, bounded griefing that extends D-C4-7c (unsolicited ENFTs); (2) `scheduleOf` grows without bound: its ABI return is **77,568 bytes at the 30M ceiling** (1211 segments) and ~**159,616 bytes at the 60M ceiling** (2493). The web indexer reads `scheduleOf` per token (automatic-payout spec §10 leaves indexer storage to the builder), so many large-schedule tokens are an off-chain memory/latency surface.
- **Sepolia block gas limit.** It could not be pinned to a single number. After the Glamsterdam upgrade (activated on Sepolia 2026-10-06) the default clients default to 60,000,000, with an optional 200,000,000 validators may set (blocks near 200M were observed); mainnet stays at 60,000,000. Per the brief this is reported against both **30,000,000** and **60,000,000**; even at a 200M Sepolia ceiling (~8,300-segment mint) the read operations stay roughly 10-15M, well under the limit, so the no-brick conclusion holds at every limit.
- **Impact in plain words:** no funds can be locked by a long schedule — a mintable token always claims and transfers. A creator can still grief a recipient with a large-schedule token (bounded extra gas to dispose of it) and bloat the off-chain indexer with an arbitrarily large `scheduleOf`.
- **Suggested fix:** cap the segment count in `_storeSchedule` (for example `if (segments.length > MAX_SEGMENTS) revert ScheduleTooLong();` with a modest MAX_SEGMENTS such as 64-256), which keeps every operation and the `scheduleOf` return O(1)-bounded; and bound the indexer's per-token read regardless.

### F-C4-2a: Dust-funded tokens drain a keeper through its own defences

- **Severity:** Low. Automatic-payout spec section 4.5 explicitly assigns the fee-coverage risk to the caller ("A caller cannot know whether it covers the fee ... that risk is the caller's"), and skip-zero (section 8) is the only spec-mandated mitigation. `KEEPER_MIN_FEE` is an implementer addition, so defeating it does not push the drain past what the spec accepts. The cost to the attacker is real but tiny against the keeper's.
- **Affected:** `keeperPlan` in `tasks/keeper.ts`: the min-fee test compares the *advertised* fee, and the skip-zero rule only matches a last payout of exactly zero. `requestPayout` pays the keeper `min(fee, payout/10)`.
- **Status: fixed after the review.** the keeper now judges the minimum fee by the expected fee. The test is now `F-C4-2a (fixed)`; the figures below are from the review. The rest of this entry describes the code as reviewed.
- **PoC test:** `FINDING F-C4-2a` in `test/redteam/keeperDrain.hh.ts`.
- **Attack:** the attacker mints N tokens to himself, funded with 100,000 units (0.0001 ETH) each, one unit per second, interval one hour, advertised fee 1 ETH. Each interval the payout is about 3,600 units (3.6e-6 ETH), the attacker is the recipient and gets 90% back, the keeper gets the capped fee of about 3.6e-7 ETH. Measured on Hardhat (gas price about 1 gwei), 3 tokens, 3 intervals, identical in all three configurations (default, `KEEPER_MIN_FEE=1e15`, `KEEPER_INCLUDE_DRY=1`):

  | | keeper gas per token per interval | keeper cost (ETH) | keeper income (ETH) | attacker cost per interval (ETH) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | interval 1 | 817,806 | 0.000818 | 0.00000036 | 0.00000036 (the fee) |
  | interval 2 | 783,976 | 0.000784 | 0.00000036 | 0.00000036 |
  | interval 3 | 782,106 | 0.000782 | 0.00000036 | 0.00000036 |

  Keeper gas is about 2,267 times the attacker's per-interval cost. **That ratio is gas-price-sensitive:** it was measured at Hardhat's gas price of about 1 gwei and grows at realistic gas prices (the attacker's cost is the fee, a share of the payout, which does not scale with gas price; the keeper's cost does). The price-independent comparison is the gas ratio: the keeper uses about 800,000 gas per token per interval. The attacker also paid one mint (about 676,000 gas) per token, which is about one interval of the keeper's spend, so over a day of hourly intervals the keeper spends roughly 25-30 times what the attacker did, over a month more than 500 times. The attacker also gets the endowment back as it pays out, so the cost of locking it is only the time value.
- **Why the defaults do not help:** skip-zero needs a zero payout and these are not; `KEEPER_MIN_FEE` is compared with the fee the owner *advertises*, not what will be paid.
- **Impact:** a keeper operator can be made to burn gas on tokens that pay it nothing worth having. No funds of anyone else are at risk.
- **Suggested fix:** the keeper already reads `lastPayoutOf`. Use an estimated fee, `min(advertised fee, last.amountWei / 10)`, for the min-fee test whenever there is a previous payout (and for never-paid tokens apply the test only after the first one). An attacker then has to pay a fee of at least the keeper's minimum every interval, which makes the attack cost the attacker what it earns the keeper.

### F-C5-5a: A stalled call stalls the whole pass

- **Severity:** Low. No funds at risk; payouts are delayed and a cron-driven keeper can pile up runs.
- **Affected:** `runKeeperPass` awaits `publicDecrypt` and `tx.wait()` with no timeout, strictly in sequence, in `finish` and in the request loop.
- **Status: fixed after the review.** each decryption and each wait for a mined transaction now times out. The tests are now the two `F-C5-5a (fixed)` cases. The rest of this entry describes the code as reviewed.
- **PoC test:** `FINDING F-C5-5a` in `test/redteam/keeperDrain.hh.ts`: the first decrypt never resolves; after 3 s, none of three requested payouts is finalised and the pass has not returned.
- **Impact:** a relayer that accepts a request and never answers (or a transaction that is never mined) blocks every token after it, and every later pass if the same token is first in the list. A pending payout that cannot be decrypted (accepted by the spec, section 9) sits first in `plan.finish` each time and blocks the rest.
- **Suggested fix:** a timeout (for example 60 s) around `publicDecrypt` and `tx.wait()` that counts the token as failed and moves on.

### F-C5-5b: One unreadable token aborts its contract's scan

- **Severity:** Low. It needs a failing read; none is reachable on-chain today (every view for a minted token succeeds, and the C4-1 schedule ceiling keeps `payoutDue` far below a node's call gas cap), but a transient RPC error or rate limit is enough, and the scan issues 4N calls in parallel.
- **Affected:** the scan `try` in `runKeeperPass` covers the whole contract, so one rejected `Promise.all` entry discards every token's state.
- **Impact:** one failing read (for example an RPC rate limit) makes the keeper skip every token of that contract for the pass, including payouts already pending and owed to their recipients; they are delayed, not lost.
- **Status: fixed after the review.** each token is read on its own. The test is now `F-C5-5b (fixed)`. The rest of this entry describes the code as reviewed.
- **PoC test:** `FINDING F-C5-5b` in `test/redteam/keeperDrain.hh.ts`: token 2's `autoPayoutOf` throws; token 1 (pending, owed) and token 3 (due) are not processed: `{ requested: 0, finished: 0, failed: 1 }`.
- **Suggested fix:** read each token in its own `try` (or `Promise.allSettled`) and plan with the tokens that were read; also finish pending payouts before the failed ones are retried.

### F-C5-5c: Scan cost grows with every token ever minted

- **Severity:** Low. Costs the keeper RPC calls (quota, time), not gas.
- **Affected:** `runKeeperPass` in `tasks/keeper.ts`: the scan makes four view calls for every token id from 1 to the token count, on every pass.
- **Impact:** anyone can mint cheap tokens to make every keeper pass slower and more RPC-hungry, which raises the odds of F-C5-5a and F-C5-5b. No funds at risk.
- **Status: reduced after the review, not fixed.** two reads per idle token instead of four (17 calls for the 8 tokens below, was 33); the scan still grows with every token minted. The test is now `F-C5-5c (reduced)`. The rest of this entry describes the code as reviewed.
- **PoC test:** `FINDING F-C5-5c` in `test/redteam/keeperDrain.hh.ts`: 8 tokens with payouts off give 33 RPC calls per pass; each token costs the attacker about 727,000 gas once and the keeper four calls on every pass for ever, increasing the chance of F-C5-5b and F-C5-5a.
- **Suggested fix:** remember tokens that are not enabled between passes within a run, read in `payoutDue` only first, or page with a cursor; and see F-C5-5b.

### F-C5-1a: A wrapper over a non-WETH token passes the deploy script's reuse path

- **Severity:** Low. It needs the owner to point `TOKEN_ADDRESS` at the wrong wrapper; no third party can trigger it. It is Low rather than Informational only because nothing can be repaired after deploying (see C5-6).
- **Affected:** `scripts/deploy.ts`: `checkWeth` (the symbol/decimals/code rule, `wethProblem`) runs only when the script deploys the wrapper itself (`TOKEN_KIND=wrapped` without `TOKEN_ADDRESS`). With `TOKEN_ADDRESS` set (and for `NFT_KIND=auto` with `TOKEN_ADDRESS`), the script reads `underlying()` from the token and calls `reusedUnderlying`, which refuses only a zero or unreadable address, or a `WETH_ADDRESS` that differs from what the token reports. `WETH_ADDRESS` set to the same non-WETH address therefore also passes. `AutoPayoutEndowmentNFT`'s constructor refuses only `underlying() == address(0)`.
- **Status: fixed after the review.** the script now applies the WETH rule to a reused token's underlying and requires `WETH_ADDRESS` on a public network. The tests are now the two `F-C5-1a (fixed)` cases. The rest of this entry describes the code as reviewed.
- **PoC test:** `FINDING F-C5-1a` in `test/redteam/deploy.hh.ts`: a `ConfidentialWETH` over a contract with symbol `MOCK` and 6 decimals is accepted by `reusedUnderlying` (with `WETH_ADDRESS` unset and set), `wethProblem` would have refused it (`is not a WETH contract`), and `AutoPayoutEndowmentNFT` deploys over it with `weth()` equal to that address. The test reproduces the script's reuse-path calls rather than running the script, because a reused token needs an existing chain.
- **Impact:** an `AutoPayoutEndowmentNFT` over a wrapper of, say, a stablecoin deploys cleanly. Minting and manual claims work. The first automatic payout burns the confidential balance through `unwrap`, and `finalizePayout` reverts at `IWETH(weth).withdraw` (analysis from reading the code, not executed: the token has no `withdraw`), so the pending payout cannot complete; anyone can finish the unwrap on the wrapper, which sends the underlying tokens to the NFT contract, which has no function to move them out. The contract cannot be fixed afterwards. Only tokens whose owner enabled payouts are affected.
- **Suggested fix:** in `scripts/deploy.ts` apply `checkWeth(underlying)` to the underlying the reused token reports (it is read-only and runs before anything is sent), and on a public network compare it with the canonical WETH address the owner pins in `WETH_ADDRESS`. Optionally also check in the contract constructor.

## By design

| ID | Behaviour | Spec reference | User-facing wording needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-C2-2 | An under-funded `mint` (or `mintAuto`) succeeds and produces a token whose endowment is zero (the token transfer is all-or-nothing, so asking for more than the creator holds moves nothing). ### Notes for Task 5 (not findings, hypotheses to test)

1. ACL is never revoked: after a transfer the previous owner can still read every `distribution` handle that was current while they owned the token. Check that none of them still holds a value that the new owner should not share, and that the handle at the moment of transfer is a pre-accrue value (so the new owner's claim is not readable by the old owner).
2. `requestDiscloseEncryptedAmount` (`T:208-216`) lets any address make a handle publicly decryptable provided both that address and cWETH hold ACL on it (`T:210`, `ACL.sol:222`). A previous owner (or a creator, donor) holds ACL on handles that the cWETH contract also holds ACL on: the creator on `received` at mint (`T:305`) and every owner/donor on their own `transferred` handles. Check whether any of those encodes a hidden amount (the endowment size is the obvious one: `received` at mint equals the endowment when the creator is not the owner, and the creator is granted on it at `T:305`; `endowmentRemaining` is a separate handle, but the amount equals it at mint).
3. The shared trivial-zero handle (`asEuint64(0)` at `ET:90`, `ET:236`) is held by many addresses; harmless, but confirms no owner-specific secret lives behind it.
4. `unwrapAmount_` is PUB, so each payout amount (before fee) is publicly decryptable by design (automatic-payout spec); confirm it is only the claimed amount and not the remaining endowment.
